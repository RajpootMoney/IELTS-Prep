# IELTS Speaking Strategies: Reaching Band 7.0+

The IELTS Speaking test is an 11 to 14-minute interview conducted in three parts. Achieving **Band 7.0** requires demonstrating fluency without unnatural hesitation, a wide lexical resource with natural idioms and collocations, clean complex grammatical structures, and clear pronunciation with natural intonation.

---

## 1. The Four Assessment Criteria

1. **Fluency & Coherence (25%):** Speaks at length without noticeable effort; uses a range of connectives; occasional hesitation to search for ideas (acceptable at Band 7), but not hesitation to search for basic grammar.
2. **Lexical Resource (25%):** Uses vocabulary flexibly; uses less common and idiomatic vocabulary naturally; can paraphrase successfully.
3. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%):** Frequently uses complex structures with good control; produces a majority of error-free sentences.
4. **Pronunciation (25%):** Easy to understand throughout; uses a wide range of phonological features (sentence stress, intonation, rhythm) effectively.

---

## 2. Part 1: The Extend-and-Enrich Technique (4–5 Minutes)

In Part 1, the examiner asks everyday questions (hobbies, work, accommodation, hometown).

### The Golden Rule: The 3-Sentence Formula
Never answer with a simple "Yes", "No", or one sentence. Always provide:
1. **Direct Answer:** Answer the question directly.
2. **Explanation / Reason:** Add "because..." or explain the context.
3. **Specific Detail / Example:** Mention an example or personal preference.

> **Examiner:** *"Do you enjoy cooking?"*  
> - **Band 5/6 Answer:** *"Yes, I like cooking very much. I cook food every day for my family."* (Too short, repetitive vocabulary).  
> - **Band 7+ Answer:** *"To be honest, I thoroughly enjoy cooking whenever my schedule permits. Preparing meals from scratch acts as a great stress reliever after a long workday, and I particularly love experimenting with traditional Indian curry recipes."*

---

## 3. Part 2: The 2-Minute Cue Card Framework (3–4 Minutes)

You have **1 minute to prepare** and must speak for **1.5 to 2 minutes continuously**.

### Preparation Strategy (The 60-Second Grid)
Do not write complete sentences. Write bullet points with **power keywords**:
```
[ Who / What ]: Name / Object
[ Where / When ]: Context / Timeline
[ What happened ]: 2 action verbs
[ Why memorable ]: 2 emotive / evaluative adjectives
```

### The Chronological Story Structure
To avoid running out of things to say at the 1-minute mark:
1. **Context & Origin (25 seconds):** How and when did this event begin?
2. **The Climax / Central Experience (45 seconds):** What specifically took place? What was the challenge or surprise?
3. **The Aftermath & Impact (30 seconds):** What happened next? How did you or others react?
4. **Reflection (20 seconds):** Looking back, why was this meaningful to you?

---

## 4. Part 3: Abstract Discussion & The PREP Method (4–5 Minutes)

Part 3 moves away from personal experiences to **broader societal, cultural, and global concepts**.

### The PREP Framework:
- **P (Point):** Direct thesis answering the examiner's conceptual question.
- **R (Reason):** Explain the sociological, economic, or psychological cause.
- **E (Example):** Provide an example from society, media, or general trends (not your personal life!).
- **P (Perspective / Alternative):** Mention how other groups or future trends view this.

> **Examiner:** *"Do you think technology makes people feel more isolated?"*  
> - **P:** *"In many respects, technology can inadvertently foster emotional isolation."*  
> - **R:** *"Although social media platforms facilitate instantaneous communication, they frequently replace meaningful face-to-face social bonds with superficial digital interactions."*  
> - **E:** *"For instance, many young professionals report spending hours scrolling through feeds rather than socializing in person, leading to feelings of detachment."*  
> - **P:** *"That said, for individuals living in remote areas or living abroad, these digital channels provide an indispensable lifeline to community support."*

---

## 5. Natural Discourse Markers & Stall Tactics

When you need 3 seconds to organize your thoughts, do **not** remain silent or say *"Uhhh"*. Use natural fillers:
- *"That’s a thought-provoking question, actually..."*
- *"Off the top of my head, I’d argue that..."*
- *"I haven't considered that in great depth before, but from my observation..."*
- *"It’s hard to generalize, but broadly speaking..."*
