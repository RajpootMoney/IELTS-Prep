# Candidate Profile & Diagnostic Baseline

## 1. Candidate Overview & Immigration Objective

- **Target Examination:** IELTS General Training (Computer-Delivered / Paper-Based)
- **Primary Objective:** Canadian Permanent Residency via Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker / Canadian Experience Class)
- **Benchmark Required:** Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) Level 9 / 10
- **Strategic Focus Realignment:** **Primary focus on WRITING and READING** (the two highest-friction modules for CLB 9). Listening and Speaking are maintained through high-efficiency targeted drills.
- **Revised Time Allocation:**
  - **Writing: 40%** (Task 1 Letter mastery + Task 2 Essay architecture, criteria-driven feedback)
  - **Reading (GT): 35%** (Closing the gap from 30/40 to 34–35/40; eliminating TFNG and Heading traps)
  - **Listening: 15%** (Section 4 maintenance, distractor immunity, high-efficiency drills)
  - **Speaking: 10%** (Fluency routines, Part 2 & 3 frameworks)
  - *Regular Working Days:* 60–90 minutes
  - *Intense Work Days (11-hr shifts):* 30 minutes minimum effective dose
  - *Weekend / Long Session:* 2–2.5 hours
  - *Rest Day:* 1 recovery day per week
- **Pedagogical Preference:** Direct, practical, examiner-style. Clear English explanations with Hinglish (*Hindi + English*) clarification for subtle nuances, grammar pitfalls, and exam traps.

## 3. Detailed Weakness Inventory

### A. Listening Diagnostic Profile
- **Section 4 Monologue Vulnerability:** Losing thread when speaker moves through 10 rapid lecture points without pauses.
- **Synonym & Paraphrase Blindspots:** Waiting for the exact word in the question prompt instead of anticipating synonyms (e.g., waiting for "cost" while audio says "financial expenditure").
- **Direction Changes & Distractors:** Getting trapped when a speaker says *"We originally planned for Friday... but due to scheduling, it had to be Monday."* (Writing down Friday instead of Monday).
- **Word-Level Hyper-Focus:** Fixating on a single unfamiliar word, causing panic and missing the next two answers.

### B. Reading (General Training) Diagnostic Profile
- **GT Scoring Misconception:** Believing 30/40 is Band 7.0 (it is only Band 6.0 in General Training).
- **True / False / Not Given Logic Trap:** Assuming unmentioned facts are true because they sound plausible in real life.
- **Matching Headings Inefficiency:** Reading entire paragraphs multiple times rather than targeting topic sentences and structural signposts.
- **Speed & Pacing:** Spending 25+ minutes in Section 1 and 2, leaving insufficient time for the more complex Section 3 passage.

### C. Writing Diagnostic Profile (To Be Calibrated in Day 1)
- *Assessment Target 1 (Letter):* Tone consistency, covering all bullet points with equal weight, proper conventions.
- *Assessment Target 2 (Essay):* Clear position maintained throughout, well-developed supporting ideas, grammatical range without forced obscure vocabulary.

### D. Speaking Diagnostic Profile (To Be Calibrated in Day 1)
- *Assessment Target:* Fluency vs. speed distinction, lexical range, structural coherence, pronunciation and intonation.

## 4. Personal Rules of Engagement

1. **Never Peek at the Answer Key Before Submitting:** An unearned 40/40 gives zero diagnostic value.
2. **Every Error Must Be Categorized:** If an answer is wrong, determine whether it was Vocabulary, Paraphrasing, Distractor, or Carelessness.
3. **Pacing Over Perfection:** Never spend more than 90 seconds stuck on a single reading question; mark an educated guess and move on.
4. **Active Correction:** Re-write or re-record every failed task using the corrected model structure.
