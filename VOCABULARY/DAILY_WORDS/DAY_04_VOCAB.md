# IELTS GT Vocabulary Bank — Day 04
### Theme: Education, Analytical Verbs & Academic Research (Section 3 L & R)

Study these 10 targeted words. Memorize the collocations, Hinglish nuance, and IELTS exam usage.

---

## 1. Hypothesis *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** A proposed explanation made on the basis of limited evidence as a starting point for investigation.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** परिकल्पना, अनुमान (Parikalpana)
- **Collocations:** `test a hypothesis, formulate a hypothesis, support the hypothesis`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The research team conducted three distinct trials to test their initial hypothesis."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Section 3 Listening (students discussing projects) and Section 3 Reading.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Plural is 'hypotheses' (spelled with -es, pronounced -eez). Traps candidates in listening!

---

## 2. Methodology *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** A system of methods used in a particular area of study or activity.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** कार्यप्रणाली, शोध पद्धति (Karyapranali)
- **Collocations:** `research methodology, robust methodology, questionable methodology`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The supervisor criticized the study's methodology because the sample size was statistically inadequate."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 3 dialogues between tutor and student.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Not just 'method' (a single technique). 'Methodology' is the overarching philosophical framework.

---

## 3. Corroborate *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To confirm or give support to a statement, theory, or finding.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** पुष्टि करना, समर्थन करना (Pushti karna)
- **Collocations:** `corroborate evidence, findings corroborate, corroborate the claim`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Subsequent laboratory experiments failed to corroborate the researcher's preliminary claims."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Reading Section 3 (academic passages) and Band 8 Writing Task 2.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Much more precise than 'prove' or 'support'. Implies independent verification.

---

## 4. Plagiarism *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The practice of taking someone else's work or ideas and passing them off as one's own.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** साहित्यिक चोरी, नकल (Sahityik chori)
- **Collocations:** `commit plagiarism, detect plagiarism, plagiarism guidelines`
- **Example Sentence:** *"University students face immediate suspension if they are found guilty of academic plagiarism."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 3 college assignment rules.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Distinct from 'copyright infringement'; plagiarism is an intellectual fraud.

---

## 5. Empirical *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Based on, concerned with, or verifiable by observation or experience rather than pure theory.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** अनुभवजन्य, प्रयोगसिद्ध (Anubhavjanya, prayog-siddha)
- **Collocations:** `empirical evidence, empirical study, empirical data`
- **Example Sentence:** *"There is substantial empirical evidence demonstrating that bilingualism improves cognitive flexibility."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Academic Reading Section 3 and high-scoring Task 2 essays.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** The opposite of 'theoretical' or 'speculative'.

---

## 6. Comprehensive *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Complete; including all or nearly all elements or aspects of something.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** विस्तृत, व्यापक, सम्पूर्ण (Vistrit, sampurna)
- **Collocations:** `comprehensive review, comprehensive approach, comprehensive training`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The department published a comprehensive report detailing national energy consumption."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening and Reading; replaces 'full' or 'complete' in Task 1 & 2.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Do not confuse with 'comprehensible' (which means easy to understand).

---

## 7. Synthesize *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To combine a number of things into a coherent whole.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** संश्लेषण करना, जोड़कर एक करना (Sanshleshan karna)
- **Collocations:** `synthesize data, synthesize information, synthesize findings`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Students are required to synthesize information from multiple academic journals for their dissertation."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Reading Section 3 and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Combine' is physical; 'synthesize' involves intellectual integration to create something new.

---

## 8. Rigorous *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Extremely thorough, exhaustive, or accurate.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** कठोर, सख्त, बारीकी से किया गया (Kathor, bariki se kiya gaya)
- **Collocations:** `rigorous testing, rigorous standards, rigorous analysis`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Before any pharmaceutical drug is approved, it undergoes rigorous clinical trials."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High-level modifier for Task 2 and Reading.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Strict' usually relates to rules/people; 'rigorous' describes the depth and precision of a process.

---

## 9. Ambiguous *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Open to more than one interpretation; having a double meaning; unclear.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** अस्पष्ट, अनेकार्थी (Aspasht, anekarthi)
- **Collocations:** `ambiguous statement, deliberately ambiguous, morally ambiguous`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The contractual wording was dangerously ambiguous, leading to a prolonged dispute between both parties."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Reading Section 3 and Listening Section 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Vague' means lacking detail; 'ambiguous' means it can be understood in two conflicting ways.

---

## 10. Curriculum *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The subjects comprising a course of study in a school or college.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** पाठ्यक्रम (Pathyakram)
- **Collocations:** `school curriculum, core curriculum, curriculum reform`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Critics argue that the modern secondary school curriculum neglects essential financial literacy skills."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Common topic in Task 2 education essays and Speaking Part 1 & 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Plural is 'curricula' (or 'curriculums'). 'Syllabus' is the outline for one specific subject; 'curriculum' is the total educational programme.
