# IELTS GT Vocabulary Bank — Day 01
### Theme: Academic & Analytical Vocabulary (High Frequency L4 / R3 / W2)

Study these 10 targeted words. Memorize the collocations, Hinglish nuance, and IELTS exam usage.

---

## 1. Substantial *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Large in size, number, value, or importance.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** काफी बड़ा, महत्वपूर्ण (Kaafi bada, mahatvapurna)
- **Collocations:** `substantial increase, substantial amount, substantial difference`
- **Example Sentence:** *"There has been a substantial increase in the number of remote employees over the last three years."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Essential in Task 2 to replace overused words like 'big' or 'a lot'. Crucial in Listening Section 4 when speakers describe statistical shifts.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Unlike 'huge' (too informal for Task 2) or 'immense' (used for physical scale/nature), 'substantial' implies measurable, concrete significance.

---

## 2. Discrepancy *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** An unexpected difference between two things that should be the same.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** विसंगति, फर्क, अंतर (Visangati, antar)
- **Collocations:** `noticeable discrepancy, discrepancy between X and Y, reconcile a discrepancy`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The committee identified a major discrepancy between the reported expenses and the actual bank transactions."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High-frequency in Listening Section 3 (academic projects) and GT Task 1 formal complaint letters.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Difference' is neutral. 'Discrepancy' implies that something is wrong, conflicting, or inconsistent.

---

## 3. Mitigate *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To make something less harmful, serious, or severe.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** कम करना, शांत करना, प्रभाव घटाना (Kam karna, prabhav ghatana)
- **Collocations:** `mitigate the impact, mitigate risks, mitigate climate change`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Implementing flexible working hours can substantially mitigate employee burnout."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Band 7+ gold-standard verb for Task 2 Problem/Solution essays and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Do not confuse with 'eliminate' (to remove completely). Mitigate means reducing the severity, not necessarily destroying it.

---

## 4. Feasible *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Possible, practical, and able to be achieved easily or conveniently.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** व्यावहारिक, संभव (Vyavaharik, sambhav)
- **Collocations:** `financially feasible, feasible alternative, feasible solution`
- **Example Sentence:** *"While solar power is environmentally desirable, installing large-scale panels may not be financially feasible for small businesses."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Ideal for Writing Task 2 when assessing proposed policies, and Task 1 when suggesting solutions to a manager.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Possible' simply means it can happen; 'feasible' means it makes practical, logical, and financial sense.

---

## 5. Prevalent *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Widespread in a particular area or at a particular time.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** प्रचलित, व्यापक (Prachalit, vyapak)
- **Collocations:** `widely prevalent, prevalent attitude, prevalent condition`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Sedentary lifestyles are increasingly prevalent among urban office workers."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Replaces 'common' or 'everywhere' in Writing Task 2 and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Popular' implies people like it; 'prevalent' simply means it exists in large numbers (can be positive or negative).

---

## 6. Detrimental *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Tending to cause harm or damage.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** हानिकारक, नुकसानदेह (Hanikarak, nuksaandeh)
- **Collocations:** `detrimental effect, detrimental to health, highly detrimental`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Excessive screen time has a demonstrably detrimental effect on children's attention spans."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High-scoring academic alternative to 'bad' or 'harmful' in Task 2 and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Dangerous' implies risk of physical injury. 'Detrimental' implies gradual corrosion, damage, or negative consequence.

---

## 7. Imperative *(adjective / noun)*
- **English Meaning:** Of vital importance; crucial.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** अति आवश्यक, अनिवार्य (Ati aavashyak, anivarya)
- **Collocations:** `it is imperative that, economically imperative, categorical imperative`
- **Example Sentence:** *"It is imperative that municipal authorities invest in clean public transit."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Perfect for recommendations and conclusions in Writing Task 2 ('It is therefore imperative that...').
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Stronger than 'important' or 'necessary'. It denotes absolute urgency.

---

## 8. Alleviate *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To make suffering, deficiency, or a problem less severe.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** राहत देना, कम करना (Rahat dena, kam karna)
- **Collocations:** `alleviate poverty, alleviate congestion, alleviate stress`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Constructing dedicated bicycle lanes would help alleviate traffic congestion in the city center."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Regularly tested in Listening Section 4 and essential for Writing Task 2 solutions.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Used specifically with negative states (pain, suffering, traffic, poverty). You cannot 'alleviate happiness'.

---

## 9. Proponent *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** A person who advocates a theory, proposal, or project.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** समर्थक, पक्षधर (Samarthak, pakshadhar)
- **Collocations:** `leading proponent, proponents of nuclear energy, staunch proponent`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Proponents of remote working argue that it boosts employee satisfaction and reduces overhead costs."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Essential for Discuss Both Views essays in Task 2 to avoid writing 'people who like this'.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Opposite is 'opponent' or 'critic'. 'Supporter' is acceptable but 'proponent' is more academic.

---

## 10. Exemplify *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To be a typical example of something, or to illustrate by giving an example.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** उदाहरण देना, मिसाल पेश करना (Udaharan dena, misaal pesh karna)
- **Collocations:** `exemplify the trend, clearly exemplify, exemplify the challenges`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Canada's points-based immigration system exemplifies a merit-based approach to international talent recruitment."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Replaces the elementary phrase 'shows an example of' in Task 2 body paragraphs.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Illustrate' paints a picture; 'exemplify' means being the absolute textbook model of a phenomenon.
