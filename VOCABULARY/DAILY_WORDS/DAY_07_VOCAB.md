# IELTS GT Vocabulary Bank — Day 07
### Theme: Week 1 Consolidation, Linking Devices & Discourse Markers

Study these 10 targeted words. Memorize the collocations, Hinglish nuance, and IELTS exam usage.

---

## 1. Notwithstanding *(preposition / adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** In spite of; despite.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** के बावजूद (Ke baawajood)
- **Collocations:** `notwithstanding the challenges, notwithstanding the fact that`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Notwithstanding the economic headwinds, the technological sector continued to generate new vacancies."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High-scoring formal concession marker for Task 2 body paragraphs.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Formal alternative to 'despite' or 'in spite of'. Can be placed at the beginning or end of a phrase.

---

## 2. Consequently *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** As a result.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** परिणामस्वरूप (Parinaam-swaroop)
- **Collocations:** `and consequently, consequently led to`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Fuel prices soared dramatically; consequently, shipping costs increased across all consumer retail goods."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Standard Band 7+ cause-and-effect transition marker (replaces simple 'so').
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Follows a semi-colon or starts a new sentence with a comma.

---

## 3. Conversely *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** Introducing a statement or idea which reverses one that has just been made or referred to.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** इसके विपरीत (Iske viprit)
- **Collocations:** `conversely, some argue, or conversely`
- **Example Sentence:** *"High interest rates encourage saving; conversely, they discourage business borrowing and capital investment."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Band 7+ contrast marker in Task 2 discussion essays.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Unlike 'however', 'conversely' specifically compares two opposing sides of a coin.

---

## 4. Furthermore *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** In addition to what has already been said; moreover.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** इसके अलावा, और भी (Iske alawa)
- **Collocations:** `furthermore, it is evident, furthermore, research indicates`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Recycling reduces landfill volume; furthermore, it conserves finite energy resources."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Used to add an additional supporting argument in Task 2 body paragraphs.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Do not use 'moreover' and 'furthermore' consecutively in the same paragraph.

---

## 5. Predominantly *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** Mainly; for the most part.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** मुख्य रूप से (Mukhya roop se)
- **Collocations:** `predominantly urban, predominantly positive, rely predominantly on`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The local agricultural economy remains predominantly dependent on seasonal monsoon precipitation."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Academic qualifier to avoid absolute over-generalizations in Task 2 and Reading.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Replaces crude generalizations like 'mostly' or 'all'.

---

## 6. Invariably *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** In every case or on every occasion; always.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** हमेशा, बिना अपवाद के (Hamesha, bina apwaad ke)
- **Collocations:** `invariably leads to, invariably results in, almost invariably`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Failing to proofread writing submissions almost invariably results in careless mechanical errors."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Strengthens conclusion statements in Task 2 and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Much more sophisticated than 'always'.

---

## 7. Subsequent *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Coming after something in time; following.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** बाद का, आगामी (Baad ka, aagami)
- **Collocations:** `subsequent research, subsequent developments, in subsequent years`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The initial trial was inconclusive, but subsequent testing confirmed the drug's therapeutic efficacy."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High-frequency in Reading chronological passages and Task 1 process letters.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Next' is casual; 'subsequent' formalizes the temporal sequence.

---

## 8. Paradoxically *(adverb)*
- **English Meaning:** In a seemingly absurd or self-contradictory way that may nonetheless be true.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** विरोधाभासी रूप से (Virodhabhasi roop se)
- **Collocations:** `paradoxically enough, paradoxically, the data shows`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Paradoxically, hyper-connectivity through social platforms has made modern citizens feel more socially estranged."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Shows high lexical sophistication and critical thinking in Task 2 and Speaking Part 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Highlights a surprising, counter-intuitive reality.

---

## 9. Ubiquitous *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Present, appearing, or found everywhere.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** सर्वव्यापी, हर जगह मौजूद (Sarvavyapi)
- **Collocations:** `ubiquitous presence, become ubiquitous, ubiquitous smartphone`
- **Example Sentence:** *"In contemporary metropolises, high-definition security cameras have become almost ubiquitous."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Advanced alternative to 'found everywhere' in modern society essays.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Stronger and more academic than 'widespread'.

---

## 10. Prerequisite *(noun / adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** A thing that is required as a prior condition for something else to happen or exist.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** आवश्यक पूर्व शर्त (Aavashyak poorv shart)
- **Collocations:** `essential prerequisite, prerequisite for success, prerequisite qualification`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Mastering IELTS listening paraphrasing techniques is an essential prerequisite for achieving Band 8.0."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Task 1 workplace letters, Task 2 education essays, and GT Reading policies.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Requirement' is general; 'prerequisite' specifically must be fulfilled BEFORE moving forward.
