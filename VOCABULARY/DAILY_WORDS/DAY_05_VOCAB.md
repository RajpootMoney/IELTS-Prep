# IELTS GT Vocabulary Bank — Day 05
### Theme: Environment, Conservation & Urban Ecology (High Frequency L4 / R3 / W2)

Study these 10 targeted words. Memorize the collocations, Hinglish nuance, and IELTS exam usage.

---

## 1. Biodiversity *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The variety of plant and animal life in the world or in a particular habitat.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** जैव विविधता (Jaiv vividhta)
- **Collocations:** `loss of biodiversity, conserve biodiversity, rich biodiversity`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Deforestation in the Amazon basin poses an existential threat to global biodiversity."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Essential in Listening Section 4 ecological lectures and Task 2 environment essays.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Broad biological concept; do not write 'variety of animals' when 'biodiversity' is available.

---

## 2. Ecosystem *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** A biological community of interacting organisms and their physical environment.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र (Paristhitiki tantra)
- **Collocations:** `fragile ecosystem, marine ecosystem, disrupt the ecosystem`
- **Example Sentence:** *"The introduction of invasive species can destabilize entire freshwater ecosystems."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 4 anchor word.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Habitat' is the home of one species; 'ecosystem' is the entire interactive network.

---

## 3. Degradation *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The process in which the quality or condition of something becomes worse.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** क्षरण, गिरावट, ह्रास (Ksharan, girawat)
- **Collocations:** `environmental degradation, soil degradation, land degradation`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Intensive agricultural practices have accelerated soil degradation across the arid plains."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Academic synonym for 'damage' or 'ruin' in Task 2 and Reading.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Implies progressive, systemic deterioration over time.

---

## 4. Deplete *(verb)*
- **English Meaning:** To use up the supply or resources of something.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** समाप्त करना, खत्म कर देना (Samapt karna, khatam karna)
- **Collocations:** `deplete natural resources, severely depleted, deplete fish stocks`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Overfishing has severely depleted bluefin tuna populations across the Mediterranean."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 4 note completion.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Noun form is 'depletion' (e.g. 'ozone layer depletion').

---

## 5. Renewable *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Capable of being replenished naturally within a human timescale.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** नवीकरणीय (Navikaraniya)
- **Collocations:** `renewable energy, renewable resources, transition to renewables`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Governments must offer tax subsidies to accelerate the transition to renewable energy sources."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Task 2 and Speaking Part 3 on energy policies.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Opposite is 'non-renewable' or 'finite' (e.g. fossil fuels).

---

## 6. Conservation *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The preservation and careful management of the environment and of natural resources.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** संरक्षण (Sanrakshan)
- **Collocations:** `wildlife conservation, energy conservation, conservation effort`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Community-led conservation efforts have helped stabilize Bengal tiger numbers in India."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 4 and Reading Section 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Preservation' means leaving untouched; 'conservation' implies sustainable, wise human management.

---

## 7. Sustainable *(adjective)*
- **English Meaning:** Able to be maintained at a certain rate or level without exhausting natural resources.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** सतत, टिकाऊ (Satat, tikaau)
- **Collocations:** `sustainable development, sustainable practices, environmentally sustainable`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Urban planners must adopt sustainable practices to prevent uncontrolled city sprawl."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Standard collocations in Task 2 and Reading Section 3.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Core global benchmark concept for Express Entry candidates.

---

## 8. Urbanization *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The process of making an area more urban; the progressive movement of populations into cities.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** शहरीकरण (Shahrikaran)
- **Collocations:** `rapid urbanization, unplanned urbanization, consequences of urbanization`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Rapid urbanization has put severe strain on municipal water and sanitation infrastructures."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** High frequency in Reading Section 3 and Writing Task 2.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** Refers to demographic movement and physical expansion of cities.

---

## 9. Contamination *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The action or state of making something impure by polluting or poisoning.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** दूषित होना, संदूषण (Dooshit hona, sandushan)
- **Collocations:** `water contamination, radioactive contamination, soil contamination`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Industrial runoff caused widespread chemical contamination of the local drinking supply."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Listening Section 4 spelling trap; Reading passages.
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Pollution' is the broader environmental state; 'contamination' implies foreign hazardous agents entering a specific substance.

---

## 10. Habitat *(noun)*
- **English Meaning:** The natural home or environment of an animal, plant, or other organism.
- **Hindi / Hinglish Nuance:** प्राकृतिक आवास (Prakritik aawas)
- **Collocations:** `natural habitat, habitat destruction, loss of habitat`
- **Example Sentence:** *"Urban expansion is encroaching upon the natural habitat of migratory birds."*
- **IELTS Exam Application:** Beware singular vs plural: 'habitat' vs 'habitats' in Section 4 listening!
- **Trap / Nuance Distinction:** 'Inhabitation' is the act of living; 'habitat' is the actual ecological terrain.
